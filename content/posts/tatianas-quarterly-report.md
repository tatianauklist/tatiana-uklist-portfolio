# Tatiana's (Slightly Delayed) Quarterly Report

Hey folks! Wow it’s been a while since I’ve written. BUT IN MY DEFENSE I’ve been incredibly busy. I’ve had a pretty hectic past three months and change so I figured I’d catch you guys up on what I’ve been up to because it’s been pretty cool not going to lie on that one.

### March - The Month I Turned Australian

March was such a fun month. It kicked off my six week trip to Australia. This was the longest I’ve spent in one of my favorite countries and continents on earth. It was SUPER jam packed and filled with a lot of really cool firsts. I landed on the weekend of the Formula One race in Melbourne so the airport and city were BUZZING. I got a fun treat when I landed and saw one of the leading Australian Football League (AFL) clubs giving interviews at the airport. I’m not an AFL fan because I genuinely don’t understand it but it was cool nonetheless. The day I landed was also the day that I went to see Linkin Park with one of my best friends which taught me that the best way to curb jetlag is to go to an incredibly noisy concert to stay awake. Later in the week, I went to see Book of Mormon for the first time with a couple friends. It was so good and funny. I highly recommend. And then I rounded out the week at one of my favorite hacker parties of the year. 

After my week in Melbourne, I headed off to Sydney for a little break for my birthday week and to speak on a panel at University of Technology Sydney’s Cyber Society about being a woman in cybersecurity. It was a really fun experience being on a panel with women involved in different aspects of the industry. I also got to meet a bunch of really cool people working at household brands like Pepsi. Since my week in Sydney coincided with my birthday week, I also took the time to try out some really really delicious food recommendations I got from my local friend and take long walks between suburbs. This wouldn’t be my last time in Sydney during the trip but we’ll touch on that in April’s section. 

After Sydney, I made my way to one of my favorite new places: Cairns. I booked this side quest on a whim because I had time to kill and honestly I wanted to do a roadtrip but the fuel prices that had just started going up made that slightly more hectic and risky. When I got to Cairns, I was greeted with the most INSANE views of mountains and greenery before seeing the ocean outside of my hotel. This place was absolutely gorgeous, and since I was here, I figured I’d check off a bucket list item and go snorkeling in the Great Barrier Reef. When I went, it was the tail end of cyclone season so the water was HECTIC, but I still saw a lot of cool animals and gorgeous scenery that’ll live on in my memory. 

My last stop in March was Brisbane. For most folks, Brisbane is where you go to go to theme parks and the beach since some of the world’s best beaches are within an hour of Brisbane proper. For me, I used Brisbane as a place to take a slight break since I was still working American hours and my sleep schedule very much reflected those 3AM calls. But I still got to wander around and see the cool stuff that Brisbane has to offer on the Brisbane River. I think next time I go back, I’ll make sure it isn’t four weeks into a six week trip where I’m working American hours for those four weeks. 

### April - TelCo’s and Haunted Venues

Now April is where it gets fun. April started my stretch of three live hacking events in three months sprint. Before I jumped into that, I went back to Melbourne to relax and finally get on a proper Australian sleep schedule. I got to explore neighborhoods I’ve never been to, eat amazing food, and play pool more times than I could count. But it was a great last week before work started officially. 

After Melbourne I went back to Sydney to put on a live hacking event with one of my favorite customers in partnership with one of the TelCo powerhouses of Australia. This was such a unique event. We were staying in Circular Quay (pronounced like key for the Americans in the room) but our testing venue was at Q-Station Manly which meant every single day started and ended with the most beautiful ferry ride on Sydney Harbour. On the way back, we were consistently seeing sunsets over the Opera House that left you speechless. The event itself truly did test me in ways I never thought possible. I had to become sharper and clearer in how I explained things because I was working with a group of people who weren’t involved in the bug bounty side of their business at all. Despite the bumps, we came out with a stronger relationship with the hackers, and both customers. I’m excited to put these learnings to the test at our next live hacking event with them. 

Once the event was done, it was time to go home. I really missed home (and my cat). AND it was time to hit the ground running for our next live hacking event in three weeks. During my down time in this period, I built the first iteration of my learning MCP called knowledge-forge that gives me custom, unique projects for where I’m at in my learning journey. Knowledge-forge came about because I couldn’t find a course or tool that taught me the way my brain needed to be taught. I’ll go more into how I built it and what the future iterations look like in another post since this is getting long and I still have two months to go haha


### May - Glizzy’s and Fancy Marriott’s

Compared to April, May was a pretty light month. I started studying for Azure certifications and had one live hacking event, one event retest, and the planning of our last one for the quarter. But it was absolutely the month that I started forgetting how back-to-back everything was. 

I started off the hecticness by traveling back to Jolon, California for one full day to do a retest for one of my favorite federal events with one of my favorite program owners. It was a short trip, but it was really nice being in a place where there’s virtually no light pollution and you can look up in the sky and see the stars. It was also the place I realized you can smell the lettuce when you drive past a lettuce farm with the windows down. 

To stick with the theme of public sector, our live hacking event for May was out in Baltimore in partnership with the State. This was an incredibly unique event because it was my first true public sector event, and our first event of the year with purely web targets. It was an event that had MANY chances to get hairy, but in the end, we ended up with over 200 submissions and zero escalations. Talk about a major win. It gave me the fuel I needed for my next event, which I only realized on Wednesday that I was leaving for the following Thursday. 


### June - The Month I Sped Down the Autobahn

June was great. It was the month I drove a Mercedes down the autobahn and found out how easy it is to get speed blindness. It was also the start of my five weeks of no trips. But before I could get to that point, I still had one last live hacking event in (you guessed it) Germany. This wasn’t my first time in Germany but it was my first time in a city that reminded me so much of Bentonville, Arkansas. The town itself was very small and 98% of the town was taken up by the one large corporation that made its headquarters there. It’s always so fascinating to see how similar and incredibly different countries can be. 

The event itself was also fantastic and came along with it’s own challenges. How do you truly prove impact when the environment is only sandboxed? How do you reconcile what an incredibly large company believes isn’t critical with something that would be critical in a production environment? And how do you have these conversations when not all the decision makers are in the same room? I really don’t have answers but it has given me so much to think about with these events moving forward. 

### Moving Forward:

While I’ve thoroughly enjoyed getting a break from all the things that have kept me busy the past few months, I’m really really looking forward to get back to the insanity of my life. I’ll be putting out a few blog pieces a week about things I’m working on, how my certification journey is going, and just being more diligent about recapping the things that are happening both personally and professionally. If you’ll be in Las Vegas for DEFCON or BlackHat, I look forward to seeing you all. And even more so, I look forward to giving my first talk for DEFCON at Policy Village (date tbd). Once the talk is over, I’ll have a blog series about the topic I’ll be speaking on and the experience as a whole. As always, thank you again for reading. I know this one was a doozy!
