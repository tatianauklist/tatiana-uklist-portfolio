## Privacy You Inherit: Part 1

### Background
It started as a rabbit hole after reading a New Yorker piece by Ronan Farrow on Sam Altman and OpenAI. That rabbit hole turned into a talk (aka my first talk) accepted through DefCon's Policy Village. And while giving the talk was fun and full of firsts, it made me realize that I can go SO much deeper when writing about it on my own time. 

The talk itself was an exploration into how the histories of cultures impacted the way those cultures viewed surveillance and privacy. While these topics have been super important for ages, the acceleration and implementation of AI have really changed the playing field when it comes to privacy and surveillance. So let's dig in, shall we?

### The Four Countries I Chose and Why
Privacy and surveillance is usually measured on a scale that ranges from collectivism to individualism. Most countries fall into a middle ground because it's incredibly rare for a country to be a true collectivist society or a true individualist society. When I started prepping for my talk, I had to be incredibly intentional about how to show the contrast in these mindsets and which countries fit within them. By the time I submitted my proposal, I landed on East Asia (with a focus on China), Germany, the Soviet Bloc, and the United States. And funnily enough, I've had personal experiences with each of the countries or regions I've chosen.

### What This Series ISN'T
The goal of this blog series isn't to lay out a massive opinion piece. Like my talk, the goal is to show how societies are shaped by their histories. How the histories of these societies impact the collective memory of those who live within them. And how these histories are still showing up in the modern day. I'm not a policy person. I'm just an outsider that noticed a pattern and wanted to show people that pattern.
