# Learning by Doing (Even When I Don't Know What I'm Doing)

Hi there 👋 my name is Tatiana and I’ve lived in a space I lovingly call “the Uncomfy Zone” for most of my career. Don’t believe me? I’m a first generation American in the most loving Soviet family who grew up in Houston. I grew up surrounded by family members in the oil industry and that was seen as the standard. And honestly what I thought my life was going to be until my sophomore year in college. 

My sophomore year in college was an incredibly pivotal point for me. I was incredibly involved in the Jewish community on campus, my grades up until that point were pretty good, and I was finding my groove on campus (insert Ray William Johnson voice) UNTIL…I started mineralogy. Mineralogy is essentially chemistry for rocks and was seen as a massive weed out class for the major. And oh man did that class break me quickly. While my classmates spent 1.5 hours twice a week absolutely enamored by the rocks and their chemical makeup, my eyes were glazed over and I was on my laptop imagining another future. So I did what any rational 19 year old did at the time, told my parents I wanted to quit the somewhat stable path I was on, change my major and work in non-profit after graduation. Luckily I’m blessed with some incredibly supportive and patient parents because after some discussion, we were able to land on a resolution: come spring, I was changing my major to economics just in case I wanted to go to law school later on. 

As fall semester turned to spring (and a near failing mineralogy grade turned into a C), I finally started this long awaited pivot. I started taking more economics classes (which funnily enough was my first introduction to programming through R), I stayed even more involved with Hillel, and I was probably the happiest I’ve been in my college experience. 

I didn’t know it at the time, but this entire experience was building my attitude and framework for what I learn, how I learn and how I was going to build my career. I saw the signal that it was time to make a change, so I followed the energy. When I followed the energy, I saw so much proof of possibility of what my future could look like which gave me permission to imagine it for myself. Once I started imagining it for myself, it felt like a whole new world opened up. I finally felt like I was uncovering a whole treasure trove and world of possibilities that weren’t visible to the naked eye. But before I could dive deep into this world of new possibilities, I had to hold myself accountable by saying it out loud. From there, I was able to map out what parts intrigued me the most and dove head first into every resource I could find. Throughout the process, I never saw myself as an expert (even if I by textbook definition I was close to it) and tried to absorb as much as possible. 

Feel free to follow along on my journey of learning out loud. I’m active on my website, LinkedIn, and Medium. I would love to connect and hear how about your journey. Until next time!
